# Class 43 HST visual research and implementation notes

## Research objective

The previous power car was recognisable as an HST, but its nose, light arrangement, grilles, cab access details, roof equipment and livery separation were still too generic. This pass used a wider set of exterior photographs and railway-history sources to identify details that remain visually important even in a browser-friendly model.

The reference images were used only as design references. They are not copied into or redistributed with the game.

## Primary historical and technical sources

1. National Railway Museum — InterCity 125: Sir Kenneth Grange
   - https://www.railwaymuseum.org.uk/whats-on/intercity-125-sir-kenneth-grange
   - Confirms 43002 as the first production Class 43 and identifies the aerodynamic wedge-shaped nose as a central Kenneth Grange design feature.

2. National Railway Museum — 40 years of High Speed Trains
   - https://www.railwaymuseum.org.uk/about-us/press-office/celebrate-40-years-high-speed-trains-national-railway-museum
   - Supports the importance of the nose-cone styling, interior layout and aerodynamics.

3. National Railway Museum — 43002 joins the collection
   - https://www.railwaymuseum.org.uk/about-us/press-office/high-speed-locomotive-sir-kenneth-grange-joins-national-railway-museum
   - Confirms the preserved power car and its long operational history.

4. National Railway Museum — 43102 record-breaking power car
   - https://www.railwaymuseum.org.uk/about-us/press-office/porterbrook-donates-record-breaking-class-43-hst-power-car-national-railway
   - Confirms the diesel speed record context and the importance of the Class 43 as a high-speed diesel design.

5. National Railway Museum — diesel and electric manuals list
   - https://www.railwaymuseum.org.uk/sites/default/files/2023-09/2023-05-12%20Diesel%20%26%20electric%20manuals%20list%20AC.pdf
   - Confirms surviving Class 253/HST power-car component and equipment documentation.

6. 125 Group, On Track magazine issue 134
   - https://www.125group.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/OTF134.pdf
   - Useful for underframe pipework, battery-box handles and restoration-detail observations.

## Photographic references reviewed

7. Front-on view of preserved 253001 / 43002
   - https://www.flickr.com/photos/66202473@N04/54105250795
   - Key observations: paired circular front lights, central grille, wide yellow warning panel, front number panel and broad horizontal colour band.

8. Three-quarter museum view of the 1975 power car
   - https://www.flickr.com/photos/64386205@N08/52734510649/
   - Key observations: nose taper, roof crown, windscreen rake, cab cheek shape and relationship between nose and engine-room body.

9. Cab-side and front-quarter detail of 43075
   - https://alongthesetracks.blogspot.com/2019/08/cab-front-photo-of-class-43075-east.html
   - Key observations: cab access door, side window, vertical handrails, lower equipment and front-side number placement.

10. Side view of a later CrossCountry Class 43
    - https://www.flickr.com/photos/stuart166axe/16495671838
    - Key observations: cab door arrangement, side glazing, modern light treatment, roof line and side-body taper.

11. Class 43 gallery with multiple operators and angles
    - https://www.tauntontrains.co.uk/photos/gallery/class43/15
    - Used to compare front, side, roof and livery variations across multiple power cars.

12. Additional Class 43 historical image collection
    - https://fmea.docbrown.info/docspics/ArchiveSteam/lococlass43.htm
    - Useful for original blue/yellow colour separation and museum-side detail.

## Visual conclusions applied to the model

### Nose and cab

- Replaced the single wedge with a multi-section lofted nose.
- Added progressive narrowing and lowering towards the nose tip.
- Added a crowned roof and cab shoulder transition.
- Split the windscreen into two panes with a central pillar.
- Added windscreen rake, wipers, side cab windows and mirrors.

### Front face

- Added paired circular light clusters on both sides.
- Added a central vertical grille.
- Added a front number panel.
- Added tail lamps, coupler hatch, coupling head and lower beam detail.

### Bodyside and roof

- Added large framed radiator/intake panels with individual slats.
- Added smaller lower service grilles.
- Added cab access doors, door glazing and vertical handrails.
- Added twin roof fan housings and exhaust outlets.
- Added underframe tank, battery-box and pipework forms.

### Running gear

- Deepened bogie sideframes.
- Added visible axles, hubs, spring forms and transverse beams.
- Retained animated wheel rotation.

### Fleet presentation

Five logo-free, historically grounded colour schemes are used across the six services:

- Original InterCity 125 blue/yellow
- InterCity Executive-inspired grey/blue/red
- Western dark green
- Cross-country maroon
- Infrastructure measurement yellow

The first train uses the preserved 43002/253001-inspired treatment and front numbering.

## Performance boundary

The model is intentionally detailed only at the existing near-train rendering tier. Distant HSTs still use the inexpensive outline representation, preventing the higher-detail power cars from being rendered across the entire city simultaneously.
